3. Async functions and setTimeout() -
    a. in ex-2, first the MEP happens
    b. b() is put into the call stack, browser's WebAPIs console does the printing, b() is popped out of the callstack
    c. setTimeout(cb) is registered by the WebAPIs of the browser with 2s timegap
    d. in gec, we've the line-16 printed by the browser's console WebAPIs, GEC is popped out of the callstack
    e. After 2 sec, the registered cb() is pushed to the 'CALLBACK QUEUE'
    f. The 'EVENTLOOP' watcher checks if the CALLSTACK is empty and then pushes the cb() from CALLBACK QUEUE to the CALLSTACK
    g. After the cb() is executed, cb() is popped out of the CALLSTACK

4. DOM APIs -
    a. First, a GEC is created and pushed to the call stack. The line-19 log is executed
    b. The DOM API 'addEventListener' WebAPI is registered by the browser with the click EVENTLOOP
    c. The line-23 log is executed and the GEC is popped while the registered WebAPI remains in the browser
    d. Whenever a click is done on the target element, the cb2() gets pushed to the CB-Q, EVENTLOOP checks if the CALLSTACK is empty and then pushes the cb2() to the CALLSTACK
    e. We may wonder why the CBQ and EVENTLOOP is needed, imagine if n-clicks are done, to keep a track of the callbacks and the order along with ensurinf the GEC is maintained intact.. Thus CBQ and EVENTLOOP are helpful
    f. The registered callbacks end at close of the browser or if we manually program them to be removed

5. Fetch APIs -
    a. First, a GEC is created and pushed to the callstack.
    b. A setTimeout WebAPI is registered by the browser with the cb3() and time 2s. Note that this cb3 is pushed to the CALLBACK-QUEUE after the time is done
    c. A fetch() WebAPI is registered by the browser with the cbf(). Note that the cbf() is pushed to the MICROTASK-QUEUE after the time is done
    d. The event loop watches the CALLSTACK until its empty. Next, it pushes the MICROTASK-QUEUE items first. After MQ are empty, the CBQ items are pushed to the CALLSTACK

6. NOTE:
    a. Priority of (MICROTASK-Q > CALLBACK-Q)
    b. Thus, if the number of MICROTASK-Q items > CALLBACK-Q items, we come across a situation called Starvation of CB-Q
